Square Root Finder

Break any number into prime factors and pair them up — each pair contributes one factor to the square root. Or squeeze two bounds until they trap the root.

Try:
The pairing method: Every prime factor of a perfect square appears an even number of times. Drag matching bubbles onto each other — each pair that snaps together contributes one factor to the root. Any bubble left without a partner means the number is not a perfect square.

The squeezing method: We know √n lies between two consecutive whole numbers. Move the blue lower bound up and the red upper bound down — whenever lo² < n < hi², the root is trapped inside. When lo and hi are just 1 apart, you have found the exact position of √n on the number line.

Why this works: Square roots are monotone — if a² < n < b² then a < √n < b. Halving the gap each time (binary search) is exactly how calculators find square roots. Each squeeze step gives you one more decimal place of precision.
